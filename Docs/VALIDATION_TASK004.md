# TASK-004 — Batted-ball flight validation (status: NOT validated)

Regenerate: explicit test `BattedBallValidationTests.WriteBattedValidationReport` → `TestResults/batted_validation.md`. Thresholds were pre-registered in the overnight ExecPlan before any replay.

## Model under test

`AerodynamicModel.BattedBall`: Nathan, *Analysis of Baseball Trajectories* (2017), Eqs. 10–11 — C_D = 0.297 + 0.0292·(ω/1000 rpm) jointly fitted with C_L = 1.12S/(0.583 + 2.333S) on 2016 Statcast (TrackMan) fly balls at Tropicana Field — plus exponential spin decay τ = 30 s (TrackMan figure quoted by Nathan; Nathan 2008 shows decay barely changes distance). Shared `BallFlightSimulator` (RK4, 5 ms, spin evaluated at each stage time); flight ends at the first ground contact.

## Data

459 balls in play from nine 2024 Tampa Bay home games at Tropicana Field (fixed dome: 72 °F, 15 ft, no wind), 5 development / 4 holdout games; `Assets/Game/Tests/Fixtures/Statcast/batted_*.csv`, regenerated by `Tools/statcast/make_batted_fixture.py`. 14 balls with repeated (imputed) EV/LA dropped. Public Statcast has no batted-ball spin, so the validation assumes backspin = 100 rpm/° × (LA − 7°) clamped to [0, 4500] rpm (medians read from Nathan 2020, Fig. 4) and no sidespin. Statcast `hit_distance_sc` (landing or catch point) vs our horizontal distance at ground contact.

## Diagnosis (not resolved)

One-parameter sweeps on the development set (diagnostics only, nothing adopted):

| change | dev primary mean / RMS | residual by LA bucket 10° → 40° |
|---|---|---|
| none | +28.8 / 32.3 ft | +33 … +24 |
| C_D × 1.15 | +3.4 / 15.6 ft | +24 … +6 (low-angle balls still long) |
| exit speed − 5 to − 6 mph | +2.9 to −2.4 / 14.7 ft | flat (+9 … −1) |
| backspin × 0.5 or × 0 | — | strong launch-angle trend; rejected |

A uniform exit-speed offset flattens the residual best; it would arise if the 2016 fit absorbed an exit-speed reading difference between the 2016 TrackMan data it was fitted to and 2024 Hawk-Eye contact-point exit speeds (earlier tracker generations under-read exit speed by ≈ 4 mph). That is a hypothesis: drag (ball lots), the spin model, and the meaning of `hit_distance_sc` for caught balls are also candidates, and this data (no spin, no hang time) cannot separate them. The pitch-side result (2024 tracking consistent with C_D 0.35 for pitches) argues against a large drag increase.

## Decision

No calibration adopted: any single correction fitted here would be an un-attributed fudge. The batted-ball flight code, lab and tests stand, but **distances are about 30 ft (≈ 8 %) long relative to 2024 Statcast** and must not drive distance-dependent gameplay (home runs, fielding range) until resolved. HittingLab and BattedBallLab display this caveat.

## Next research

Batted-ball data with spin and hang time (Hawk-Eye per-ball spin, or published 2023–2024 analyses), a Hawk-Eye-era refit of C_D/C_L for batted balls, checking exit-speed definitions between tracking systems, and comparing caught vs landed balls separately.
